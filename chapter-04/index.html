LeRobot · SO-101 · Chapter 4

Program it, or Teach it?

Before we dive into training, one big question: why use AI at all? Robots have been doing useful work for decades without it. The secret every engineer knows is that AI isn't always the answer — it's a tool you reach for when a task gets too messy to spell out in rules. This chapter builds that instinct: meet the other ways to control a robot, then see exactly when learning wins.

Part 1 · The Big Question

Two ways to give a robot a “brain”

There are really only two ways to make a robot do something:

📝 Program it

You write the exact rules: "move here, then there, if this then that." You are in full control. The robot only ever does what you spelled out.

Great when the task is exact and predictable.

🧠 Teach it

You show examples and let the robot figure out the rules itself — including things too fuzzy to write down. That's learning / AI.

Great when the task is messy and varied.

The whole chapter in one sentence: use the simplest method that does the job — and only climb toward AI when the world gets too messy for rules. 🪜

Part 3 · See The Difference

Playback vs. Learned

Here's the difference you can feel. Both arms were "taught" to grab a cube in the middle. Now move the cube and run them. The playback robot blindly repeats its motion; the learned robot actually finds the cube.

Part 4 · Why Not Just Write Rules?

The wall that rules hit 🧱

"Fine," you say, "I'll just write more rules — one for every cube position." But the real world is endless:

  • The cube can be at any position and angle. 🧊
  • The lighting changes; shadows move.
  • Some objects are soft — a towel or cable has infinite shapes you can't list.
  • Just finding the cube in a grid of camera pixels is nearly impossible to hand-code.

Add a rule for each case and your program explodes into millions of branches — and some things (“grasp any crumpled towel”) you can't put into words at all.

The key realization: when you can show a task far more easily than you can describe it, stop writing rules and start collecting examples. That's the door to AI. 🚪

Part 6 · The Honest Answer

AI isn't “better” — it's the right tool for messy jobs

 Program it (rules / control)Teach it (AI)
Best forexact, repeatable, describablemessy, varied, hard to describe
Costcheap, no data neededneeds data + computing
Reliability100% predictableusually great, not guaranteed
Explainable?yes — you wrote every stepharder — it learned its own way
Handles surprisesonly ones you foresawgeneralizes to new ones
So why does this course go the AI way? Because the SO-101's jobs — grab a cube that's anywhere, on a real table, under changing light — are exactly the messy kind. Playback and rules hit the wall; learning is the tool that fits. Now you know why we train. 🧠

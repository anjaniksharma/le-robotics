From One Task to Many

The ACT policy you trained is a specialist: one task, no words. But what if you could just tell the robot what to do — "put the red cube in the box" — and it understood? That's a VLA: Vision + Language + Action. And instead of training from scratch, you start from a foundation model that already knows about pictures and words, then teach it your setup. Meet SmolVLA.

Part 1 · The Specialist

ACT is brilliant — but narrow

The policy from Chapters 5–7 does exactly one thing — the task you demonstrated — and it has no idea what words mean. Want a second task? Record a whole new dataset and train a whole new policy.

Humans aren't like that. You can be told "grab the blue one instead" and just… do it. To get robots closer to that, we need a model that takes a language instruction as input and can handle many tasks. That's the leap this chapter makes.

The dream: one robot brain you can talk to — "pick up the cube," then "now put it on the shelf" — no retraining between commands. 🗣️

Part 2 · The Big Idea

A VLA: Vision + Language + Action

A VLA takes three inputs and turns them into motion. The new one is language — the instruction literally steers what the robot does.

The magic: change only the words — keep the same cubes, same camera — and the robot does something different. The instruction is part of the input, so it changes the output. 🪄
Part 3 · The Shift

Don't train from scratch — fine-tune

Here's the second big idea. Remember how GPT (Chapter 6) is pretrained on mountains of text, then adapted to specific jobs? SmolVLA does the same for robots: it's a foundation model (450M parameters, lerobot/smolvla_base) already pretrained on lots of images, language, and robot data.

So you don't start from zero — you fine-tune: take the pretrained brain and nudge it with just ~50 episodes of your own task. It arrives already knowing what a cube is and what "in the box" means.

pretrained foundation model + a little of your data → your robot fine-tuning, not training from scratch
Analogy: training ACT from scratch is like teaching someone to cook from zero. Fine-tuning SmolVLA is hiring an experienced chef and just showing them your kitchen. Way less to teach. 👩‍🍳

Part 4 · Under the Hood (lightly)

How SmolVLA is wired

Just two big pieces — you already know both ideas:

  • A vision-language model (SmolVLM) — "eyes + words." It looks at the camera images and reads the instruction, and understands the scene together with the request. (Same family as the text model from Chapter 6, plus vision.)
  • An action expert — takes that understanding (plus the joint state) and produces the action chunk (Chapter 6's ACT idea).

It generates the motion by refining random noise into a smooth action — a bit like a sculptor starting from a rough block. You don't need the details; the takeaway is: understand the scene & words → produce motion.

📷 images  +  💬 instruction
↓
SmolVLM — vision-language "understanding"
↓   (+ 🔢 joint state)
Action expert — turns understanding into motion
↓
🎯 action chunk → the motors

Part 5 · Many Tasks, One Brain

The same model, many instructions

Because the instruction is an input, one fine-tuned SmolVLA can do several tasks — you just say which. Try switching the order without touching anything else. 👇

Same scene, same brain — pick a task.

How the data is built

To teach several tasks, your dataset just includes episodes of each task, every one labelled with its own instruction (the task field from Chapter 7). SmolVLA learns to tie each instruction to the right behavior.

Data tip from the SmolVLA paper: record ~50 episodes and repeat each variation several times. They used 5 cube positions × 10 episodes; a 25-episode version was too few. Quality and quantity still rule (Chapter 7). 📊

Notice: the arm, cameras, and motors never changed across these tasks — only the sentence you gave it. That's the whole promise of language-conditioned robots.
Part 6 · Do It For Real

Fine-tuning SmolVLA

It's the Chapter-8 pipeline with a foundation model as the starting point. Two extra ideas: install the SmolVLA extra, and point --policy.path at the pretrained model instead of training a blank one.

1 · Install the SmolVLA dependencies

pip install -e ".[smolvla]"

2 · Fine-tune the pretrained model on your dataset

lerobot-train --policy.path=lerobot/smolvla_base \ --dataset.repo_id=$HF_USER/mydataset \ --batch_size=64 --steps=20000 \ --output_dir=outputs/train/my_smolvla --job_name=my_smolvla \ --policy.device=cuda --wandb.enable=true

~20k steps ≈ 4 hours on one A100 GPU — a great fit for the cloud training from Chapter 8 (Colab or HF Jobs). No local GPU needed.

3 · Run it on the arm — now the instruction matters

lerobot-rollout --policy.path=$HF_USER/my_smolvla \ --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=/dev/tty.usbmodemXXXX \ --robot.cameras="{front:{type:opencv,index_or_path:0,width:640,height:480,fps:30}}" \ --task="Put the red cube in the box" --duration=60

Change --task to give a different order — same policy, different behavior.

Part 7 · Which To Use?

ACT vs. SmolVLA

Neither is "better" — they're different tools. Pick by what your project needs.

 ACTSmolVLA
Starting pointtrained from scratchpretrained foundation model, fine-tuned
Languagenonetakes a text instruction
Tasks per modelonemany
Sizesmall & fastlarger (~450M)
Data neededa full dataset per task~50 episodes to adapt
Best forone fixed, well-defined taskseveral tasks, or "talk to it" control
Rule of thumb: one repetitive job on a bench → ACT is simple and quick. Want to give orders in words, or handle several tasks → SmolVLA. Both ride the same SO-101 you built. 🤝
